## Contracts & Agreements (Prompts 1–8)

### 1. The Freelance Services Agreement
```
You are a commercial contracts attorney with 15 years drafting agreements that hold up when relationships don't. Draft a freelance services agreement for [SERVICE TYPE] between [CLIENT NAME] and [CONTRACTOR NAME]: [PROJECT SCOPE, TIMELINE, FEE]. Include: (1) the services section — deliverables defined specifically enough to prove scope creep, (2) payment terms: the fee structure, invoicing cadence, late interest, the kill fee, (3) IP ownership: the default — work transfers on full payment — plus the portfolio-use license most freelancers actually need, (4) termination: for convenience with notice, for cause, and what happens to work-in-progress, (5) the independent-contractor status clause, (6) the dispute resolution and governing law for [STATE]. Format: numbered sections with a defined-terms block at top. Plain-language drafting: every sentence a non-lawyer party can read and understand what they agreed to.
```

### 2. The Contract Red-Flag Review
```
Act as a contract review specialist doing the review you'd pay $500/hour for — flagging what hurts [PARTY NAME], the party receiving this review. Review: [PASTE CONTRACT TEXT]. Output: (1) the issue list — each flag with the clause reference, the exact language quoted, the risk level (high/medium/low), and who bears the risk, (2) the missing clauses: the protections this contract should have and doesn't — indemnification, limitation of liability, insurance, dispute mechanics, (3) the unfair terms: the ones that would bite in a bad year — unlimited liability, one-sided termination, auto-renewal traps, (4) recommended revision language for each red flag — written as the actual replacement clause. End with the verdict: sign, negotiate, or walk — and the 3 asks that change the verdict most.
```

### 3. The Right NDA for the Situation
```
You are a transactions lawyer who knows the NDA is where confidentiality goes to die — drafted wrong. Write a non-disclosure agreement for [SCENARIO: employee onboarding, vendor partnership, investor discussion, prospective acquirer]: [WHAT'S BEING SHARED]. (1) First decide: mutual or one-way — state which fits this scenario and the reasoning, (2) the definition of confidential information: the strong version — specific categories, not "all information" — with the carve-outs that make it enforceable: public, already-known, independently developed, (3) the obligations: non-use, non-disclosure, standard of care, (4) term: the confidentiality period that matches the information's shelf life — trade secrets vs. pitch decks need different clocks, (5) remedies: the acknowledgment that damages are inadequate — injunctive relief — without the void-as-penalty liquidated damages amateurs reach for, (6) the return/destruction obligation on termination. Format: the full NDA in standard structure.
```

## Legal Research & Analysis (Prompts 9–16)

### 9. The Case IRAC Summary
```
You are a legal researcher who briefs cases so thoroughly the associate stops hunting for the original. Summarize [CASE NAME OR CITATION]: the case that addresses [LEGAL ISSUE AREA]. (1) The IRAC: Issue — the precise question the court answered, Rule — the holding, stated as the legal principle, Application — how the court applied it to the key facts, Conclusion — the disposition. (2) The key quotes: the 2-3 passages worth citing, marked. (3) How it applies to [FACT PATTERN] in [JURISDICTION]: the analogous facts, the distinguishing facts — where my situation differs, and what that does to the prediction. (4) The plain-English takeaway: one paragraph a client could read. Caveat: verify citations independently — AI can misstate holdings; treat this as a research map, not authority.
```

### 10. Litigation Element Mapping
```

For each element: (1) the definition as [STATE] courts state it, (2) the evidence needed to prove it: documents, testimony, records — the specific items, (3) the admissibility note: the authentication or foundation each piece needs — hearsay risks, business-records exceptions, (4) the proof burden and the standard. Output: the element table — element, evidence, admissibility, status — then flag the weakest element: where this case most likely fails, and the motion the defense will aim at it. Then the fix: what evidence would shore it up and whether it's obtainable. Format: table plus the strategy note. Verify the elements against current [STATE] law.
```

### 11. Regulation Comparison Analysis

### 12. The Legal Memorandum
```
Act as a legal writing expert whose memos answer the question asked — with the reasoning shown. Draft a legal memorandum analyzing whether [FACT PATTERN] constitutes [LEGAL ISSUE] under current [STATE/FEDERAL] law. Structure: (1) Question Presented — the specific question, in one sentence, (2) Brief Answer — yes/no/probably, with the decisive reason, up front, (3) Facts — the relevant facts only, neutrally stated, (4) Discussion — the rule statement: current law, cited; the analysis: application to each material fact, including the facts that cut against the conclusion; the counterarguments: the strongest opposing reading and why it loses, (5) Conclusion with the practical next step. Verify citations — this is a draft for attorney review, not a final work product.
```
