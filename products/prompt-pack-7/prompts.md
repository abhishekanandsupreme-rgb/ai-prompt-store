## Content Calendars & Strategy (Prompts 1–8)

### 1. The 30-Day Calendar
```
You are a social media strategist who builds calendars that get published, not admired. Build a 30-day content calendar for [BRAND] on [PLATFORM] targeting [AUDIENCE] with the goal [GOAL — followers, leads, sales]. Mix: 40% educational, 25% engagement (polls, questions), 20% behind-the-scenes, 15% promo. Output a table: date | content pillar | format | hook (write the actual first line — not a placeholder) | caption summary | CTA. Include: 2 flex days per week for reactive content, the posting times for [TIMEZONE/REGION] audience, and the weekly production ritual — the 2-hour block that produces a week of content. End with the kill criterion: what to drop first when there's only 30 minutes in a week.
```

### 2. Channel Prioritization
```
Act as a channel prioritization expert. My business is [BUSINESS TYPE] with [HOURS/WEEK] available for social. Compare Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, X, YouTube, and Pinterest for my audience [AUDIENCE]: for each — where my audience actually spends time (with the reasoning by demographic and behavior), content fit (does my product/service show well there?), and the time cost per week to do it properly. Recommend a PRIMARY and a SECONDARY channel only — with the reasoning — and tell me what to ignore without guilt: the platforms that would take 6+ months to matter for [BUSINESS TYPE] and aren't worth the diverted hours. Include the audit date: when to reassess (90 days) and the metric that justifies a channel switch.
```

### 3. Brand Voice Across Platforms
```
You are a brand voice translator. My brand voice is [VOICE — e.g., playful, expert, warm]. Show me exactly how it sounds on each platform I use: [LIST PLATFORMS]. For each platform: (1) 2 example captions for the same [ANNOUNCEMENT] — rewritten natively, not pasted, (2) do/don't examples — the 3 specific things that work and the 3 that break the voice there, (3) the emoji policy (how many, which ones — the platform's tolerance is real), and (4) the tone adaptation for that platform's culture: LinkedIn = professional-casual, X = compressed wit, Instagram = visual-first caption brevity, TikTok = unfiltered. End with the 3-line voice card per platform I can pin for whoever posts.
```

### 4. Content Pillar Architecture
```
Act as a content pillar architect. Design 4–5 content pillars for [BRAND] in [NICHE]: for each — the pillar name (memorable, 2–3 words), the audience pain it serves, 5 post ideas under it, and the ratio per week. Rules: pillars must balance value-giving and offer-making (not 4 pillars of tips and 1 of sales — the mix that trains followers to never buy), and each pillar must be explainable in one sentence. Present as a reusable planning grid: pillar | pain | post ideas | weekly ratio. Then the growth logic: which pillar earns follows, which earns trust, which earns money — and what happens if any one of the three is missing.
```

### 5. Competitor Presence Analysis
```
You are a social media competitive analyst. Analyze [COMPETITOR]'s presence on [PLATFORM]: their posting cadence, top-performing content themes (from [WHAT YOU CAN SEE — engagement patterns, format choices]), engagement style, and offer strategy. Then tell me: (1) the 3 things they do that I should NOT copy — because it works for THEIR position (audience size, brand recognition, budget) and would fail for mine, (2) the gap they leave open — the audience need they systematically ignore, with the evidence, and (3) my angle to own that gap: the positioning that's credible for me, not aspirational. End with the 30-day plan to be visibly the best answer to that gap in [NICHE].
```

### 6. The 3-Week Product Launch
```
Act as a launching strategist. Plan the social launch of [PRODUCT] over 3 weeks for [AUDIENCE]: Week 1 awareness — teasers and problem content (make the problem felt before naming the solution), Week 2 launch — announcement, demos, social proof, the offer stated plainly, Week 3 sustain — FAQs, UGC, last call (real deadline only if real). Output the day-by-day plan: for each post — the day, the platform ([PLATFORMS]), the format, the specific goal (awareness/consideration/conversion), the hook, and the CTA. Include the launch-day sequence hour by hour, and the 2 contingency plans: the "launch flopped" day-3 pivot and the "launch sold out" moment plan. One narrative across all posts — the same story, told differently per day.
```

### 7. Seasonal Content Plan
```
You are a seasonal content planner. Build my [QUARTER/SEASON] content plan for [BRAND]: (1) the key dates and holidays that FIT [NICHE] — not force-fit (list them with the relevance reasoning; flag the ones every brand does anyway), (2) 5 seasonal content series ideas — recurring formats tied to the season's mood, (3) the evergreen content that runs between the seasonal pushes — the always-relevant posts that keep the account alive in quiet weeks, and (4) last [SEASON]'s audit: the 3 questions to answer from last season's data (what overperformed, what underperformed, what the audience asked for) and how this plan responds. ### 8. Small-Budget Allocation

## Copywriting & Captions (Prompts 19–25)

### 19. The 10-Caption Set
```
You are a caption copywriter. Write 10 captions for [POST — product photo, milestone, tip] in [BRAND VOICE]: 5 short (under 150 characters — the hook is everything at this length) and 5 longer storytelling (hook, 2–3 beats, payoff). Each uses a different hook type across the set: question, bold statement, story open, list tease, direct callout. Rules: the first line earns the second (feeds truncate — write for the cutoff), line breaks for scannability, 1–2 emoji max, 3–5 niche hashtags at the end (not generic). Mark the strongest 2 and explain why (the mechanism, not taste: what scroll behavior they exploit). Then the fill-in template version of each hook type — so I can generate my own next time.
```

### 20. Hook Laboratory
```
Act as a hook specialist. My posts die in the first line. Write 20 scroll-stopping first lines for [TOPIC/PRODUCT] across 5 categories: curiosity gap (the open loop the brain must close), bold claim (the defensible heresy), story (the in-progress moment: "So I almost deleted the whole account yesterday…"), specific number (the concrete: "7 requests, 4 no's, 1 yes"), and direct address ("You're posting daily and growing 2/month. Here's the actual problem."). Then TEACH me: the pattern behind each category — the formula written out — so I can generate my own. Include the test for each hook: does it work out of context (no photo, no prior post)? If not, it's a caption, not a hook.
```

### 21. CTA Ladder
```
You are a CTA copywriter. My posts get engagement but no action. Write 15 CTAs for [DESIRED ACTION — save, share, comment, click, buy, DM] ranging from soft to hard: soft ("Save this for when you need it"), medium ("Share this with someone building [X]"), hard ("DM me [WORD] — 2 spots left this month"). Organize by funnel stage: awareness CTAs (follow, save), consideration CTAs (comment, share, click), conversion CTAs (buy, book, DM). For each: the placement rule — where in the caption it belongs (mid-caption for engagement, end for conversion, never stack two asks), and the friction note (every CTA costs effort — the softer the relationship, the softer the ask). Then the fix for my real problem: the one CTA I should use for the next 30 days and why.
```

```

### 35. Social A/B Testing
```
You are an A/B testing planner for social. My hypothesis: [e.g., video outperforms carousels for reach in my niche]. Design the test: (1) what to hold constant — same topic angle, same time slot, same hashtags (every difference confounds the result), (2) the 2 variants — one variable only: the format (or the hook, or the CTA — never all three), (3) the run length: how long given [POSTING CADENCE] — enough posts per variant for a pattern (say the number and why), (4) what result confirms or denies — with the realistic margin (social noise is huge; a 15% difference may be nothing — say what a REAL difference looks like), and (5) the follow-up test — what the next experiment is if this one succeeds, and if it fails. Be honest about what platform algorithms make hard to test reliably (the non-deterministic feed is a confound no design removes).
```
