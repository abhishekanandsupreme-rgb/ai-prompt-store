## Advertising Copy (Prompts 1–8)

### 1. The 5-Variant Ad Set
```
You are a direct response copywriter with 20 years and $50M in tracked ad spend behind you. Write 5 Facebook ad variations for [PRODUCT] targeting [AUDIENCE] with the pain point [PAIN POINT]. Each ad: an attention-grabbing hook (first line does 80% of the work — write it before the body), a 2–3 sentence body that agitates honestly then resolves, and a CTA. Rotate angles across the set: one fear-based, one aspiration-based, one curiosity-based, one social-proof-led, one direct-comparison. For each variant: the recommended headline character count and the specific objection it pre-handles. All claims must be true for [PRODUCT] — no invented urgency, no miracle language.
```

### 2. Responsive Search Ad, Complete
```
Act as a Google Ads specialist managing six-figure monthly spend. Write a responsive search ad for [KEYWORD/INTENT] with 10 headlines (max 30 characters each) and 4 descriptions (max 90 characters each). Mix angles: feature, benefit, urgency (honest — only if a real deadline exists), proof, and risk-reversal. Keyword appears in at least 3 headlines; one headline uses a number; one handles the main objection. Then: which headline should win the auction for a [BUYER STAGE] searcher and why, the expected CTR range for this keyword's intent class, and the 5 negative keywords to add so this campaign stops paying for the wrong clicks.
```

### 3. Click-to-Conversion Drop Diagnosis
```
You are a performance marketer who audits funnels for a living. My [PLATFORM] ad for [PRODUCT] has a CTR of [PERCENT]% but converts at [PERCENT]%. Diagnose the 5 most common causes of this click-to-conversion drop for this product category — check: message mismatch (ad promises X, page delivers Y), page speed, offer clarity, price shock, and trust gaps. Rank by likelihood given my specifics, and for each: the exact fix and the revised ad or landing page element. Then tell me the test order — what to fix first with the reasoning (biggest suspected leak, cheapest test). End with the benchmark: what conversion rate is normal for [CATEGORY] at this price point.
```

### 4. Short-Form Video Ad Scripts
```
Act as a TikTok creative strategist who has made ads people don't skip. Write 3 short-form video ad scripts (30–45 seconds) for [PRODUCT] aimed at [AUDIENCE]. Each script: a hook in the first 3 seconds (write 2 alternative hooks per script — the hook is everything), problem agitation in under 10 seconds, a product demo moment that SHOWS the benefit rather than describing it, and a CTA that names one action. Include on-screen text suggestions and a shot list a solo creator can film. The 3 scripts must use different structures: POV, problem-solution, and pattern interrupt. Note for each: the scroll-stopping mechanism and why it works for [AUDIENCE].
```

### 5. Landing Page Copy, Structured to Convert
```
You are a landing page copywriter who writes like a helpful expert, not a hype merchant. Write the copy for a landing page selling [PRODUCT] at $[PRICE] to [AUDIENCE]: hero headline (outcome-focused, 10 words or fewer) + subhead (mechanism + timeframe), 3 benefit blocks each with proof placeholder, an objection-handling section answering the 4 real hesitations of [AUDIENCE], and a closing CTA with risk-reversal. Mark with [VISUAL] and [TESTIMONIAL] where each belongs. Rules: every section must make sense alone (readers skim), no adjective that a number couldn't replace, and the CTA copy must be specific ("Start the free 14-day trial") not generic ("Get started"). Tune total copy length to the price point — a $[PRICE] product needs proportionate proof.
```

### 6. Abandoned Cart Sequence
```
Act as an email retention specialist. Write a 5-email abandoned cart sequence for [STORE TYPE] selling [PRODUCT]: emails at 1h, 24h, 48h, 72h, and 7 days. Each: subject line, preview text, a 100-word body, and one CTA. Email 1 is a helpful nudge (checkout issue?), 2 handles the top objection ([TOP OBJECTION — e.g., price/fit/trust]), 3 is social proof, 4 introduces a real incentive [ONLY IF ONE EXISTS — never fake scarcity], 5 is the honest last call. After the sequence, explain the psychology of each step in one line (timing, message match, escalation logic). State the realistic recovery rate for [CATEGORY] and the point at which more emails cost more in unsubscribes than they earn.
```

### 7. Ad Testing Matrix
```
You are an ad copy tester who runs structured experiments, not vibes. Create a testing matrix for [PRODUCT] ads: 3 hooks, 3 bodies, 3 CTAs. Show: (1) how these combine into a structured A/B plan — which variable to test FIRST and why (test the biggest lever, one variable at a time), (2) which 3 combinations to run in the first wave, (3) the sample size each cell needs for significance at a [BASELINE]% conversion rate — show the math, and (4) the decision rule to kill or scale a variant (e.g., "kill if no conversions at 2× expected CPA at N clicks"). Include the timeline: how many days this takes at [DAILY BUDGET] and when to declare inconclusive and move on.
```

### 8. Brand Response Copy, Three Lengths
```
Act as a brand response copywriter for the moments that matter. Write copy for [SCENARIO — e.g., price increase announcement, outage apology, competitor comparison backlash] to [AUDIENCE]. Three versions: short (social, under 280 characters), medium (email, 150 words), long (blog post, 400 words). Same core message across all three: what happened, why, what we're doing, and what it means for the customer. Tone: accountable and confident — no corporate apology theater, no minimization. Include a FAQ block of the 4 questions customers will actually ask, answered honestly. Then list the 3 sentences that must NOT appear anywhere (the defensive clichés every brand reaches for under pressure).
```

## Brand & Positioning (Prompts 9–16)

### 9. Positioning Platform
```
You are a brand strategist in the April Dunford school of positioning. Develop a positioning platform for [BUSINESS NAME], a [CATEGORY] for [AUDIENCE]: the positioning statement, 3 pillars that prove it, brand personality (5 adjectives with a do/don't example each), voice guidelines with rewrite examples (here's generic copy → here's ours), and a tagline (3 options, 5 words or fewer). Differentiate sharply from [TOP COMPETITORS] who position on [THEIR POSITIONING] — name what they can't follow us into and why. Then the stress test: which pillar is weakest and what evidence would strengthen it.
```

### 10. Naming Session, 20 Candidates
```
Act as a naming consultant who has named 100+ products. Generate 20 name candidates for [PRODUCT/COMPANY] in [CATEGORY] targeting [AUDIENCE]. Organize into 4 families: descriptive (says what it does), evocative (suggests the feeling), invented (coined, ownable), and compound (two real words, new meaning). For your top 5, check: pronunciation ease (say it aloud test), domain likelihood (realistic, not "probably taken"), trademark risk (flag anything close to [KNOWN MARKS] — always verify with counsel), and a one-line rationale. Recommend one. Then the honest note: the naming criteria most founders overweight (uniqueness) and the one they underweight (memorability after hearing once).
```

### 11. Messaging House
```
You are a messaging architect. Build a messaging hierarchy for [PRODUCT]: the primary message (the ONE thing — 12 words or fewer), 3 supporting messages (each provable), and proof points for each (numbers, demos, guarantees, or logos). Then translate the hierarchy for 3 audiences: [AUDIENCE 1], [AUDIENCE 2], [AUDIENCE 3] — same truth, different emphasis: what leads for each and what drops out. Present as a one-page message house diagram in text (roof = primary message, pillars = supporting messages, foundation = proof). End with the mismatch test: where my current marketing would fail against this house — the message I'm sending that this structure says to retire.
```

### 12. Brand Voice Guide
```
Act as a brand voice designer who makes voice operational, not aspirational. Define the voice of [BRAND]: 3 voice principles (each with do/don't examples), a vocabulary list — 25 words we use, 25 we avoid (with the reason per avoided word), 3 sample rewrites showing generic copy → our copy, and how the voice flexes across surfaces: social (looser), email (medium), support (warmer), legal/compliance (neutral) — with one example sentence per surface. The test: a new hire should be able to write on-brand copy from this guide alone. Include the "voice drift" check — the 5 questions to ask monthly to detect when the voice has quietly slipped.
```

### 13. Category Creation Assessment
```
You are a category creation strategist who advises honestly — most category plays fail. My [BUSINESS] does [CATEGORY] but competes in a red ocean. Assess whether a new category frame is viable: (1) the market shift that makes it timely (a real change in buyer behavior or technology — not just my desire), (2) the new category name candidates (5), (3) the enemy — the old way of doing things that the category replaces, (4) the 12-month plan to own the narrative (content, alliances, analysts, lighthouse customers), and (5) the honest risks: the failure rate of category creation, the budget it actually takes, and the "fast follower" problem — a bigger competitor just adopting my language. If the assessment says don't, tell me what to do instead.
```

### 14. Rebrand Plan
```
Act as a rebranding consultant. My [BUSINESS TYPE] has evolved from [OLD FOCUS] to [NEW FOCUS] and our brand no longer fits. Plan the rebrand: (1) what changes — name, look, voice — each with a keep-or-change recommendation and reasoning, (2) what STAYS for continuity (the assets customers recognize us by), (3) the stakeholder-by-stakeholder comms plan: customers, team, partners, press — each with the message and the sequence, (4) the rollout order over 90 days (what launches when), and (5) the announcement post (150 words, honest about why, no "exciting new chapter" clichés). Include the cost map: every surface the old brand lives on — the places rebrands forget (email signatures, invoices, packaging, domain redirects).
```

### 15. Brand Consistency Audit
```
You are a brand audit expert. Here is my current marketing: [PASTE EXAMPLES OR DESCRIBE]. Audit consistency across 6 dimensions: visual identity, tone, promise, audience focus, proof, and differentiation. Score each 1–10 with the evidence — quote or point to specific pieces. Then: the 3 fixes that will most improve trust (ranked by impact on how professional we look to a new visitor seeing us for the first time), the pattern behind the inconsistencies (usually: no single owner of the brand), and the one-page brand checklist to prevent drift going forward. Be specific — "tone is inconsistent" is not a finding; "support emails sound like a lawyer, ads sound like a hype account" is.
```

### 16. Mission Messaging Without Greenwashing
```
Act as a mission-driven brand advisor. My [BUSINESS TYPE] wants to lead with [MISSION — e.g., sustainability, inclusion, giving back] without greenwashing accusations. Build the messaging framework: (1) the claims we can make TODAY — each mapped to the proof we hold (certification, data, third-party), (2) the claims we cannot make yet, and what evidence would unlock each, (3) the transparency language — how to talk about the gap between where we are and the mission (honesty converts better than perfection), and (4) the anti-greenwash test: the 5 questions a skeptical journalist would ask, and whether our current answers hold. Include the messaging hierarchy for [MISSION] claims: lead with the strongest-evidence claim, never the biggest-sounding one.
```

### 21. Case Study from Facts
```

## Growth, Analytics & Optimization (Prompts 35–44)

### 35. Growth Model & Funnel Leaks
```
You are a growth marketer who builds models before running experiments. My [BUSINESS TYPE] gets [TRAFFIC/SIGNUPS] per month and converts at [PERCENT]%. Build the growth model: (1) the funnel stages with current numbers — traffic → signup → activation → retention → revenue, (2) the 3 biggest leaks with the reasoning (a leak is where the biggest absolute number of qualified people drops), (3) 5 experiments to fix them, ranked by ICE score (impact/confidence/effort — show each score component), and (4) the instrumentation needed to read results — what event to track and where. End with the honest note: which leak I probably can't fix yet because it's upstream of marketing (product or positioning).
```

### 36. CRO Hypothesis Set
```
Act as a CRO specialist. My [PAGE TYPE] converts at [PERCENT]%. Propose 7 testable hypotheses for this page in this niche — each with: the psychological principle at work (loss aversion, social proof, cognitive load...), the specific change, the expected lift range (with the reasoning — where similar changes land), and the priority. Rules: test one variable at a time; the hypothesis must state what result would FALSIFY it. Then write the full copy for the winner's variant — the complete replacement section, ready to paste. Include the sample size math for [TRAFFIC]/month and which hypotheses aren't testable at my traffic (say so honestly — underpowered tests are worse than no tests).
```

### 37. Channel Portfolio Analysis
```
You are a marketing analyst. I spent $[AMOUNT] last month: [PASTE CHANNEL BREAKDOWN]. Analyze: (1) CAC by channel — with the attribution caveat per channel (last-click lies differently for each), (2) contribution margin after CAC per channel, (3) which channel to DOUBLE vs. KILL — with the confidence level and what data would make you flip the call, and (4) what data I'm missing to make a confident decision — the one metric to add this month. Present as an executive summary table with the 3 decisions it supports. Then the sequencing: this month's reallocation vs. next quarter's, and the guardrail (min ROAS per channel) that triggers automatic re-review.
```

### 38. Retention Program Design

Write the copy for the one highest-impact trigger (email or in-app). Include the honest benchmark: retention for [CATEGORY] and the point where retention is a product problem, not a marketing problem.

### 39. Funnel Copy Audit
```
You are a funnel copy auditor. Here is my current funnel: [DESCRIBE — ad → page → signup → activation]. Audit each step's copy against its ONE job per step: the ad earns the click, the page earns the signup, the signup earns activation, activation earns the habit. Find: (1) message mismatches between steps — where step N promises something step N+1 doesn't immediately confirm, (2) the weakest transition — where the biggest drop happens and what the copy does to cause it, and (3) rewrite that transition — full replacement copy, labeled with the conversion principle it applies (congruence, commitment consistency, clarity). Include what to A/B test vs. what to just fix outright (low-confidence tests on obviously broken copy waste time).
```

### 40. Attribution Reality Check
```

End with the decision rule: what result should change my budget, and by how much.
```

### 42. A/B Test Design, Honest
```
Act as an experiment designer. I believe [HYPOTHESIS — e.g., adding video will lift conversion]. Design the A/B test: (1) the primary metric — one, named, (2) the minimum detectable effect I should realistically power for, (3) the required sample size given [TRAFFIC] — show the math and the test duration in days/weeks, (4) the guardrail metrics — what could improve while something important silently breaks (e.g., conversion up, refund rate up), and (5) the pre-registered decision rule — written BEFORE the test: what result means ship, iterate, or kill. Then the honesty check: at my traffic, is this test feasible? If not, tell me what to run instead — the higher-traffic page, the bigger change, or the qualitative substitute.
