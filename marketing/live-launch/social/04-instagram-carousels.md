# Instagram Carousel Scripts — 3 Ready-to-Design

Format rules: 6–10 slides per carousel, first slide = the hook (must work as a standalone tile), last slide = soft CTA. Every slide ≤ 40 words. Caption carries the depth + prompt text (Instagram users copy from captions). No fake stats, no "AI will replace you" bait. Design: plain type on solid background, no clipart.

**Posting window:** Tue–Thu 11 AM–1 PM or 6–8 PM US Eastern. One carousel per day max.

---

## CAROUSEL 1 — "The Friday prompt" (post Day 2)

**Slide 1 (hook tile):**
> The AI prompt I run every Friday.
> It asks me one question my to-do list never does.

**Slide 2:**
> I've tried most productivity apps.
> The one ritual that stuck is one prompt and 20 minutes.

**Slide 3:**
> The prompt:
> "Act as my weekly review partner. I'll paste my notes from the week."

**Slide 4:**
> "1. Summarize what moved forward vs. what stalled.
> 2. Identify the one decision I'm avoiding.
> 3. Draft next week's top 3 priorities."

**Slide 5:**
> Why line 2 is the trick:
> Your to-do list only organizes what you've ALREADY decided to do.
> It can't see what you're avoiding deciding.

**Slide 6:**
> Is it magic? No.
> It's one honest 20-minute meeting with yourself, where the machine takes the minutes.

**Slide 7 (CTA tile):**
> Save this for Friday.
> Full collection of my recurring-work prompts → link in bio.

**Caption (carries the copyable prompt):**
> One prompt, 20 minutes, every Friday. 📝
>
> Copy it:
> "Act as my weekly review partner. I'll paste my notes from the week: [PASTE NOTES]. 1) Summarize what moved forward vs. what stalled. 2) Identify the one decision I'm avoiding. 3) Draft next week's top 3 priorities based on the above."
>
> Line 2 is uncomfortable every single time — and right almost every single time. My to-do list organizes decided work; this one question surfaces the undecided kind.
>
> Honest note: this didn't 10x anything. It's a boring, compounding habit: fewer avoided decisions, a shorter stalled-list each week.
>
> I keep a whole rotation of prompts like this for recurring work (content, email, code, planning) — link in bio if you want the rest. But this one's the flagship, and it's yours.
>
> What's the one question you'd add to a Friday review? 👇

**Note:** link in bio = landing page URL. Do not use link stickers to the store from this carousel — keep IG as top-of-funnel only.

## CAROUSEL 2 — "5 prompts that survived a year" (post Day 4)

**Slide 1 (hook tile):**
> 5 AI prompts that survived a full year in my weekly rotation.
> (I deleted dozens.)

**Slide 2:**
> My test: if a prompt survives 4 weeks of real use, it becomes a template.
> These passed 52.

**Slide 3:**
> #1 The script template
> "Write a 10-minute script about [TOPIC] with a hook, intro, 3 main points, and a CTA. Audience: [WHO]. Tone: [TONE]."
> Upgrade: ask for 3 hook options. Pick one. Rewrite the intro around it.

**Slide 4:**
> #2 The debug prompt
> "Debug this code: [CODE + FULL ERROR]. Here's what I already tried: [LIST]. Expected behavior: [WHAT IT SHOULD DO]."
> The "already tried" line skips every obvious suggestion.

**Slide 5:**
> #3 The welcome sequence
> "Write a 5-email welcome sequence for [BUSINESS TYPE]. Tone: match this: [YOUR OWN WRITING]."
> "Match this" beats every tone adjective.

**Slide 6:**
> #4 The decision matrix
> "Create a decision matrix for [DECISION]. Criteria: [WEIGHTED]. Present as a table — and tell me where the weighting is doing all the work."
> The last part is a built-in bias check.

**Slide 7:**
> #5 The Friday review
> Weekly. One uncomfortable question: "Identify the one decision I'm avoiding."
> (Full version in my last post — save both.)

**Slide 8 (CTA tile):**
> The common thread: [BRACKETS] force context.
> Context is the difference between generic output and useful output.
> More → link in bio.

**Caption:**
> Not every prompt deserves to survive. 🧹
>
> My rule: 4 weeks of real use, or it's deleted. These five passed a full year.
>
> All five in copyable form:
> 1. "Write a 10-minute script about [TOPIC] with a hook, intro, 3 main points, and a call to action. Audience: [WHO]. Tone: [TONE]."
> 2. "Debug this code: [CODE + FULL ERROR]. Here's what I already tried: [LIST]. Expected behavior: [WHAT IT SHOULD DO]."
> 3. "Write a 5-email welcome sequence for [BUSINESS TYPE]. Tone: match this: [PASTE 2-3 SENTENCES OF YOUR OWN WRITING]."
> 4. "Create a decision matrix for [DECISION]. Options: [LIST]. Criteria: [WEIGHTED]. Present as a table and tell me where the weighting is doing all the work."
> 5. "Act as my weekly review partner. 1) What moved vs stalled. 2) The one decision I'm avoiding. 3) Next week's top 3."
>
> The pattern is the point: placeholders are forcing functions. You can't skip context that has a labeled hole.
>
> I sell the full rotation (500+ across 10 themes) — link in bio. But these five are the survivors that matter, and they're yours.
>
> Which prompt have you re-typed the most this year? 👇

## Cross-use notes

- Carousel 1 and 2 share the Friday prompt with r/productivity, X Thread 1, LinkedIn Post 1, and dev.to — one asset, five surfaces, zero contradictions. This is deliberate: cross-platform consistency is what makes claims survive scrutiny.
- Do NOT put Gumroad links in IG captions/stories as raw URLs (not clickable, looks spammy). "Link in bio" only, pointing at the landing page.
- Saves (not likes) are the metric that matters for carousels — each slide deck ends with a "save this" micro-instruction on the CTA tile. Save-rate ≥ 3% of reach = strong; repost a variant of that angle later.
