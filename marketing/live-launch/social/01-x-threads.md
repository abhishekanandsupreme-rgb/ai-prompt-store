## THREAD 1 — "The 5 prompts I actually reuse" (post Day 1, Tue)

**Tweet 1 (standalone value — the hook IS a prompt):**

> The AI prompt I run every Friday costs nothing and does more for my productivity than any app I've paid for:
>
> "Act as my weekly review partner. I'll paste my notes from the week. 1) Summarize what moved vs. what stalled. 2) Identify the one decision I'm avoiding. 3) Draft next week's top 3 priorities."
>
> The 2nd line is the whole trick. 🧵

**Tweet 2:**

> Why "the one decision I'm avoiding" works: your to-do list only organizes what you've already decided to do. It's structurally blind to the stuff you haven't decided yet. Asking an AI to hunt for it in your week's notes surfaces it anyway.

**Tweet 3:**

> Second prompt, from the same rotation — the debugging one. If any of your work touches code:
>
> "Debug this code: [CODE + FULL ERROR MESSAGE]. Here's what I already tried: [LIST]. Expected behavior: [WHAT IT SHOULD DO]."
>
> The last two lines skip all the obvious suggestions you already ruled out.

**Tweet 4:**

> Third: the script/content template (videos, long posts, whatever you make):
>
> "Write a 10-minute script about [TOPIC] with a hook, intro, 3 main points, and a call to action. Audience: [WHO]. Tone: [TONE]."
>
> Follow-up that upgrades it: "give me 3 hook options for the first 15 seconds" → pick → rewrite the intro around your pick.

**Tweet 5:**

> Fourth: the email welcome sequence:
>
> "Write a 5-email welcome sequence for [BUSINESS TYPE]. Product: [ONE-LINE]. Who it's for: [AUDIENCE]. Tone: match this: [PASTE 2-3 SENTENCES OF YOUR OWN WRITING]."
>
> "Match this" is what keeps AI email from sounding like AI email.

**Tweet 6:**

> Fifth: the decision matrix, for choices bigger than a to-do item:
>
> "Create a decision matrix for [DECISION]. Options: [LIST]. Criteria: [WEIGHTED]. Present as a table AND tell me which option wins — and where the weighting is doing all the work."
>
> That last clause catches you weighting the criteria to get the answer you already wanted.

**Tweet 7 (CTA):**

> Common thread in all 5: the placeholders force you to hand over context (audience, constraints, what you tried) that you'd otherwise skip — that's where generic AI output comes from.

## THREAD 2 — "Technique vs. template" (post Day 3, Thu)

**Tweet 1 (standalone value):**

> Unpopular take from a year of prompting daily: the prompt-engineering techniques everyone debates (role, chain-of-thought, magic phrases) almost never survive contact with a Monday morning.
>
> What survives: dumb fill-in-the-blank templates for recurring tasks. Here's the difference. 🧵

**Tweet 2:**

> A technique is "add role + context + constraints + examples + output format." All correct. But every Monday you're re-deriving it from scratch for the same 6 tasks, and you skip half of it because you're busy.

**Tweet 3:**

> A template is the technique, pre-baked for one recurring task, with holes where your context goes:
>
> "Write a 10-minute script about [TOPIC] with a hook, intro, 3 main points, and a CTA. Audience: [WHO]. Tone: [TONE]."
>
> Same structure. But now it's 10 seconds to deploy, and you never skip the context part.

**Tweet 4:**

> The test I use: if a prompt has survived 4+ weeks in my rotation, it graduates to a template file. Everything else was technique-flavored procrastination.

**Tweet 5:**

> Where templates break: one-off tasks. You can't template "respond to this angry client email." Technique still rules there. The mistake is treating one-offs like recurring work and recurring work like one-offs.
