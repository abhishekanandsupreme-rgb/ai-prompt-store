## Slide 2 — WHAT IS PROMPT ENGINEERING?

**Headline:**
What is prompt engineering?

**Body:**
Prompt engineering is simply the art of writing clear instructions for AI.

Think of it like talking to a very smart intern who has zero context about your life. The more specific you are, the better the results.

**Good news:** You don't need to be a developer. You just need to be specific.

**Visual:**
Person giving clear instructions to another person.

## Slide 3 — THE 3-PART FORMULA

**Headline:**
The 3-part formula

**Body:**
Every good prompt has 3 parts:

**1. Role:** Who should the AI be?
**2. Context:** What does it need to know?
**3. Task:** What exactly do you want?

**Example:**
> Act as a fitness coach (Role).
> I'm a beginner who can only work out 20 minutes a day at home (Context).
> Write a 4-week bodyweight workout plan (Task).

**Visual:**
Three blocks labeled Role, Context, Task with arrows.

## Slide 4 — STEP 1: START WITH A ROLE

**Headline:**
Step 1: Start with a role

**Body:**
The first words of your prompt should tell the AI who to be.

**Roles to try:**
- Act as a [copywriter / teacher / coach / editor / designer / analyst]
- You are a [senior / expert / beginner-friendly] [ROLE]
- Pretend you're [PERSONA] giving advice

**Why it works:**
AI has been trained on millions of examples of these roles. Setting the role activates the right "mode."

**Visual:**
Role cards: Copywriter, Teacher, Coach, Designer.

## Slide 5 — STEP 2: GIVE CONTEXT

**Headline:**
Step 2: Give context

**Body:**
Context is everything the AI needs to know but doesn't.

**Include:**
- Your goal or situation
- Your audience (if relevant)
- Constraints or limitations
- What you've already tried (if fixing something)

**Example:**
> Context: I'm launching a newsletter for indie hackers. My audience is technical but not interested in "hustle culture." I've already written 3 emails and got a 35% open rate.

**Visual:**

## Slide 6 — STEP 3: BE SPECIFIC ABOUT THE TASK

**Headline:**
Step 3: Be specific about the task

**Body:**
Don't say: "Write something about productivity."

Do say: "Write a 200-word LinkedIn post about morning routines for remote workers. Include 3 actionable tips and end with a question."

**Specificity checklist:**
- Format (blog, email, tweet, script)
- Length (word count or time)
- Structure (sections, bullet points)
- Tone (professional, casual, funny)
- Output (table, list, essay)

**Visual:**

## Slide 7 — COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

**Headline:**
Common mistakes beginners make

**Body:**
❌ One-sentence prompts
❌ No role or context
❌ Asking for everything at once
❌ Forgetting to specify the format
❌ Not reviewing or editing the output
❌ Using the same prompt for every task

**Fix:** Use the 3-part formula + specificity checklist every time.

**Visual:**
X marks next to each mistake.

## Slide 8 — YOUR FIRST PROMPT EXERCISE

**Headline:**
Try this right now

**Body:**
Pick a task you need to do today. Write a prompt using the 3-part formula.

**Example task:** Write a Monday morning motivation post.

**Your prompt:**
> Act as a motivational coach (Role).
> My audience is remote workers who struggle with Monday motivation (Context).
> Write a 100-word Instagram caption that reframes Monday as a fresh start. End with a question (Task).

Now test it. See what you get. Edit until it sounds like you.
