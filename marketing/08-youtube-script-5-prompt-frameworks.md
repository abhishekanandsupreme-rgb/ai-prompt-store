## [0:30-1:30] THE PROBLEM WITH VAGUE PROMPTS

**[VISUAL: Side-by-side comparison. Left: "Write a blog post about dogs" → bad output. Right: Structured prompt → good output.]**

**HOST:**
Here's a real example. If I type into ChatGPT:

> Write a blog post about dogs.

I get a generic, encyclopedia-style post. It's boring. It's useless.

But if I use a framework:

> Act as a dog behaviorist.
> Audience: First-time puppy owners who are frustrated with chewing.
> Task: Write a 500-word blog post titled "Why Your Puppy Chews Everything (And 3 Ways to Stop It)."
> Structure: Problem, why it happens, 3 solutions, reassurance.
> Tone: Empathetic, slightly funny, practical.

Now I get something I'd actually publish.

The framework isn't magic. It's just giving the AI enough constraints to do good work.

## [1:30-3:00] FRAMEWORK 1: Role + Context + Task

**[VISUAL: Animated diagram showing the 3 parts: Role, Context, Task. Text on screen for each.]**

**HOST:**
Framework number one: Role + Context + Task. This is the foundation of every good prompt.

**Role:** Tell the AI who it is.
**Context:** Give it the background information.
**Task:** Tell it exactly what to do.

**Example:**
> Role: Act as a senior product manager at a SaaS startup.
> Context: We're launching a new feature and need to prioritize bugs.
> Task: Create a bug triage framework that scores each bug on: user impact, frequency, and fix complexity. Output as a table.

Why this works: The AI stops guessing and starts performing from a specific perspective.

---

## [3:00-4:15] FRAMEWORK 2: Few-Shot Prompting

**[VISUAL: Show "Input → Output" pairs. Like teaching by example.]**

**HOST:**
Framework two: Few-shot prompting. This is where you show, not just tell.

Give the AI 2-3 examples of the input/output you want, and it will match your style and structure.

**Example:**
> Write Instagram captions in this style:
>
> Input: "New coffee shop downtown"
> Output: "Your morning just got an upgrade. This spot on 5th makes oat milk lattes that taste like warm hugs. Tag a friend who needs this today."
>
> Input: "Yoga studio opening Saturday"
> Output: "Stretch into the weekend. Free classes all Saturday to celebrate their new studio. Your spine will thank you."
>
> Now do the same for: "Local bookstore hosting author signing"

The AI doesn't just give you a caption—it gives you *your* caption.

---

## [4:15-5:15] FRAMEWORK 3: Chain-of-Thought

**[VISUAL: Host on camera. Text overlay: "Show your work."]**

**HOST:**
Framework three: Chain-of-thought. For complex problems, ask the AI to reason step by step before giving the final answer.

**Example:**
> Should I switch from my part-time job to freelance full-time?
>
> Before giving me the final answer, walk me through your reasoning step by step. Think about my current income, client pipeline, emergency savings, and industry stability. Explain your logic.

This prevents the AI from jumping to conclusions. You get transparent, auditable reasoning instead of a random opinion.

---

## [5:15-6:15] FRAMEWORK 4: Constraint + Format

**[VISUAL: Show a prompt with a "constraints" box and "output format" box.]**

**HOST:**
Framework four: Constraint + Format. AI gets creative when you give it guardrails.

**Example:**
> Write 5 subject lines for a Black Friday email campaign.
> Constraints:
> - Keep each under 50 characters
> - Include emoji in at least 3
> - Avoid spam words like "free" and "act now"
> - Make them curiosity-driven, not discount-focused
>
> Output format: Numbered list with 1-sentence explanation for each.

Without constraints, you get 5 lines of "Buy now!" With constraints, you get strategic, on-brand options.

---

## [6:15-7:00] FRAMEWORK 5: Self-Review Prompt

**[VISUAL: Show a response, then a critique, then an improved version.]**

**HOST:**
Framework five: The self-review prompt. After getting an initial response, ask the AI to critique its own work.

**Example:**
> [PASTE YOUR INITIAL PROMPT AND RESPONSE]
>
> Now act as a critical editor. Review your own response and identify:
> 1. What's missing or underdeveloped?
> 2. Where could I add more specificity?
> 3. What objections might a skeptical reader have?
>
> Then rewrite the response addressing these issues.

This single technique turns a "B+" response into an "A+" response. The AI finds its own blind spots.

## [7:00-7:45] PUTTING IT ALL TOGETHER

**[VISUAL: Show a "power prompt" that combines all 5 frameworks.]**

**HOST:**
Here's a power prompt that combines all five frameworks:

> Act as a direct-response copywriter (Framework 1: Role).
> Context: I sell AI prompt packs on Gumroad to creators (Framework 1: Context).
> Task: Write a 1,500-word blog post about writing better AI prompts (Framework 1: Task).
>
> Structure: Hook, 7 frameworks with examples, toolkit section, CTA.
>
> Example style I want: [PASTE A SAMPLE PARAGRAPH YOU LIKE] (Framework 2: Few-shot).
>
> Before giving me the final draft, walk me through your outline and explain why you chose this structure (Framework 3: Chain-of-thought).
>
> Constraints: Every section needs a specific example. No generic advice. Under 200 words per section (Framework 4: Constraint + Format).
>
> After writing, critique your own draft using Framework 5 (Framework 5: Self-Review).

Try this with your next prompt. The quality jump is immediate.
