## Slide 2 — MISTAKE 1: ONE-SENTENCE PROMPTS

**Headline:**
1. One-sentence prompts

**Body:**
Bad: "Write a blog post about dogs."

Good: "Act as a dog behaviorist. Write a 500-word post titled 'Why Your Puppy Chews Everything.' Structure: Problem, why it happens, 3 solutions, reassurance."

**Why it matters:**
AI responds to structure. One sentence = one generic paragraph.

**Visual:**
A long, rambling sentence crossed out, replaced by a structured prompt.

## Slide 3 — MISTAKE 2: NO ROLE OR CONTEXT

**Headline:**
2. No role or context

**Body:**
AI is a generalist. You need to tell it what hat to wear.

Bad: "What's a good marketing strategy?"

Good: "Act as a B2B SaaS marketing director with a $5k/mo budget. I'm launching a project management tool. Give me a 90-day marketing plan focused on content and LinkedIn."

**Visual:**
Actor costume icons: "generic AI" vs. "SaaS marketing director."

## Slide 4 — MISTAKE 3: IGNORING EXAMPLES

**Headline:**
3. Ignoring examples (few-shot prompting)

**Body:**
Show, don't tell.

Bad: "Write a good Instagram caption."

Good: "Write captions in this style:
Input: 'New coffee shop downtown'
Output: 'Your morning just got an upgrade...'

Now do the same for: 'Local bookstore signing.'"

**Why it matters:**
AI matches patterns. Give it a pattern to match.

**Visual:**
Input/Output pair shown like a training example.

---

## Slide 5 — MISTAKE 4: ASKING FOR EVERYTHING AT ONCE

**Headline:**
4. Asking for everything at once

**Body:**
Bad: "Write a full marketing plan, 10 social posts, and a website."

Good: Break it into steps.
Step 1: "Write a 90-day marketing plan outline."
Step 2: "Based on this plan, write 5 social post concepts."
Step 3: "Write the first Instagram caption from concept 2."

**Why it matters:**
You can't review and refine a 5,000-word dump. Iterate in steps.

**Visual:**
A giant overwhelming document crossed out, replaced by 3 small cards.

## Slide 6 — MISTAKE 5: NO CONSTRAINTS

**Headline:**
5. No constraints

**Body:**
AI gets creative when you give it guardrails.

Bad: "Write some headlines."

Good: "Write 10 headlines. Constraints: Under 60 characters. Mix curiosity, list, and social proof formats. Avoid generic words like 'amazing' and 'ultimate.'"

**Visual:**
A "Constraints" box showing bullet points.

---

## Slide 7 — MISTAKE 6: SKIPPING THE REVIEW

**Headline:**
6. Skipping the review

**Body:**
AI writes well, but not perfectly.

Always:
1. Read it out loud (stumbles = rewrite)
2. Cut 20% (AI writes too many words)
3. Add one specific detail (a number, a story, a name)
4. Check for AI phrases ("delve into," "multifaceted," "in today's landscape")

**Visual:**
Proofreading checklist with 4 checkmarks.

---

## Slide 8 — MISTAKE 7: USING AI FOR VOICE

**Headline:**
7. Using AI for brand voice

**Body:**
AI can mimic a voice, but it can't create yours.

Bad: "Write all my social media content for me."

Good: "Write 5 caption drafts in the tone of [YOUR VOICE]. I'll edit them to match my actual style."

Your brand voice = your competitive advantage. Don't outsource it.

**Visual:**
Microphone icon with a "human" badge.

---

## Slide 9 — MISTAKE 8 & 9 & 10 (COMBO)

**Headline:**
8. Not testing prompts + 9. Forgetting the goal + 10. Ignoring the output

**Body (3 mini-cards):**

**8. Not testing prompts**
Test every prompt 2-3 times. Rewrite until the output is consistently good.

**9. Forgetting the goal**
Start every prompt with: "I want [OUTCOME] for [AUDIENCE]." If you don't know the goal, the AI won't either.

**10. Ignoring the output**
Read every AI response critically. If you wouldn't say it, don't post it.
